01 · The gist

An IOU for a dollar, written as a token.

A stablecoin is a crypto token designed to keep a steady price, almost always 1 token = 1 US dollar.

The essence: a stablecoin is a ledger entry on a blockchain plus a promise of redemption. Someone holds real dollars in a bank or in Treasury bills, and a smart contract tracks who owns claims on those dollars. Mint when dollars come in, burn when dollars go out. Everything else is detail.

On chain

A token contract

Usually an ERC-20 contract: a map of address → balance, plus transfer, mint and burn. Only the issuer may mint or burn.

Off chain

A reserve

Cash and short term government debt held by regulated custodians. The reserve is what makes the token worth a dollar.

The peg

Arbitrage

If the token trades at $0.99, traders buy it and redeem it for $1.00. At $1.01 they mint new tokens and sell. Redemption keeps the price near $1.

Flavors

Four kinds

  • Fiat backed: USDC, USDT, PYUSD
  • Crypto backed: DAI / USDS, over collateralized
  • Commodity backed: PAXG (gold)
  • Algorithmic: no hard reserve, TerraUSD collapsed in 2022
contract Stablecoin {
    mapping(address => uint256) public balanceOf;
    address public issuer;

    function mint(address to, uint256 amount) external {
        require(msg.sender == issuer);
        balanceOf[to] += amount;
    }

    function burn(uint256 amount) external {
        balanceOf[msg.sender] -= amount;
    }

    function transfer(address to, uint256 amount) external {
        balanceOf[msg.sender] -= amount;
        balanceOf[to] += amount;
    }
}

Why it matters

It moves at blockchain speed, 24/7, across borders, and any program can hold it or move it. Real stablecoins add blacklisting, pausing, upgradeable proxies, permits (gasless approvals) and role based minters, but this is the core.

The hard part is not the code. It is the bank accounts, audits, licenses and redemption operations behind it.

02 · Toolchain

Foundry builds it. Anvil runs it locally.

Foundry is a fast, Rust based toolkit for Ethereum smart contract development, started by Paradigm. Tests are written in Solidity itself.

forge

Build, test, deploy

Compiles Solidity, runs unit and fuzz tests, measures gas, and deploys with scripts. Think cargo for contracts.

anvil

Local Ethereum node

A dev chain on localhost:8545, chain id 31337, 10 funded accounts, instant blocks. It can fork mainnet so you test against the real USDC contract.

cast

Swiss army CLI

Call contracts, send transactions, decode data, convert units, query balances, from the terminal against any RPC.

chisel

Solidity REPL

Type Solidity line by line and see results. Useful to explore types and snippets fast.

Alternatives: Hardhat (JavaScript / TypeScript) is the other popular toolkit. Foundry wins on speed and on keeping everything in Solidity.

sol! {
    #[sol(rpc)]
    contract IERC20 {
        function balanceOf(address owner) external view returns (uint256);
    }
}

#[tokio::main]
async fn main() -> anyhow::Result<()> {
    let provider = ProviderBuilder::new()
        .connect_http("https://eth.llamarpc.com".parse()?);
    let usdc = IERC20::new(
        address!("A0b86991c6218b36c1d19D4a2e9Eb0cE3606eB48"), provider);
    let holder: Address = address!("0000000000000000000000000000000000000001");
    let raw: U256 = usdc.balanceOf(holder).call().await?;
    println!("{} USDC (6 decimals)", raw);
    Ok(())
}

A sensible Rust stack

  • Contracts in Solidity, tested with forge
  • Local chain with anvil, forking mainnet when needed
  • Backend in Rust + alloy to mint, transfer and index events
  • revm to simulate before sending
  • Need Solana? Anchor + SPL Token

Note: USDC uses 6 decimals, so 1000000 means 1 USDC. ETH uses 18.

04 · The issuer

USDC is the token. Circle is the company behind it.

USDC (USD Coin) is a fiat backed stablecoin redeemable 1:1 for US dollars. Circle Internet Group issues it.

History

From Centre to Circle

Launched in 2018 by the Centre consortium (Circle and Coinbase). Centre wound down in 2023 and Circle took full control. Circle listed on the NYSE in 2025 under CRCL.

Reserves

Cash and T-bills

Backed by cash at banks and short dated US Treasuries, much of it in the Circle Reserve Fund managed by BlackRock. Monthly third party attestations are published.

Business model

Interest on reserves

Holders get no yield. Circle earns the interest on the reserve assets and shares part of it with distribution partners such as Coinbase.

Multi chain

Native on many chains

Ethereum, Solana, Base, Arbitrum, Optimism, Polygon, Avalanche and more. CCTP moves USDC across chains by burning on one and minting on the other, no wrapped tokens.

On Ethereum

  • Address: 0xA0b86991c6218b36c1d19D4a2e9Eb0cE3606eB48
  • An upgradeable proxy in front of Circle's FiatToken implementation
  • Admin powers: mint, burn, blacklist, pause, set minters
  • Supports permit (EIP-2612) and transferWithAuthorization (EIP-3009) for gasless flows

USDC vs USDT

USDT (Tether) is bigger by supply and dominant on Tron and in emerging markets. USDC positions itself as the regulated, transparent option, strong in US and EU (MiCA compliant). The US GENIUS Act (2025) created a federal framework for payment stablecoins.

Circle also builds Arc, its own L1 chain tuned for stablecoin payments, with USDC as the gas token.

Centralization trade-off: USDC is a blockchain token, but it is not decentralized money. Circle can freeze any address and can upgrade the contract. You trust Circle and its banks, the chain just moves the claims.

05 · Keys, not coins

A wallet holds no money. It holds a key.

Is it a file? Sometimes the key is stored in a file. Is it a link to a hash? The address is derived from a hash, but it is an identifier, not a pointer.

Short answer: a wallet is a private key (a random 256 bit number) plus software that signs with it. Your balance is not in the wallet. It is a number stored on chain, inside the USDC contract's balanceOf map, keyed by your address. Whoever has the private key can move it.
Private key32 random bytes · secret
→secp256k1
Public key64 bytes · shareable
→keccak256, last 20 bytes
Address0x + 40 hex chars
The secret

Private key

64 hex characters. Signs transactions. Lose it and funds are gone. Leak it and funds are stolen. There is no "forgot password".

Human backup

Seed phrase

12 or 24 words (BIP-39). One seed deterministically derives many keys (BIP-32/44, path m/44'/60'/0'/0/0 for Ethereum). Back up the words, recover every account.

Where it lives

So, a file?

Software wallets store an encrypted keystore JSON file or encrypted seed. Hardware wallets (Ledger, Trezor) keep the key in a chip and never export it. Custodial wallets (an exchange) keep it for you.

Next gen

Smart accounts

With account abstraction (ERC-4337, EIP-7702) the account is a contract: multisig, spending limits, social recovery, and paying gas in USDC.

06 · The language

Solidity is to the EVM what Java is to the JVM.

Solidity is a statically typed, curly brace language for writing smart contracts. It was proposed by Gavin Wood in 2014 and is developed by a team at the Ethereum Foundation.

.sol sourceSolidity
→solc
Bytecode + ABIEVM opcodes · JSON interface
→deploy tx
Contractruns on every node's EVM
Ethereum

The platform

A network of nodes that agree on state. It ships a virtual machine, the EVM, that executes bytecode and charges gas for every operation.

Solidity

One language for it

The most used language that compiles to EVM bytecode. Ethereum does not run Solidity, it runs bytecode. Alternatives: Vyper (Python like) and Yul (low level).

Portable

Any EVM chain

The same Solidity contract deploys to Base, Arbitrum, Optimism, Polygon, BNB Chain, Avalanche C-Chain, or a private chain. This is why USDC exists on so many networks.

Gotchas

Code is law

Deployed code is immutable (unless behind a proxy), public, and holds money. Reentrancy, access control and integer bugs cost real funds. Audit, fuzz, and use OpenZeppelin libraries.

07 · Your own chain

Yes: Ethereum the software runs anywhere.

"Ethereum" means two things: the open source protocol and software (EVM, clients), and the public mainnet (chain id 1). You can run the first without the second.

OptionWhat it isConnected to mainnet?
Local dev chainAnvil or Hardhat on your laptop, throwaway stateNo (can fork a snapshot of it)
Public testnetsSepolia, Hoodi: real networks, valueless ETH from faucetsNo, separate chains
Private / permissionedHyperledger Besu with QBFT or IBFT 2.0, known validators, enterprise networksNo, fully yours
Layer 2 rollupOP Stack, Arbitrum Orbit, ZK stacks: your own EVM chain that posts data and proofs to EthereumYes, settles to it
Other EVM L1sBNB Chain, Avalanche, Polygon PoS: independent networks running the EVMNo, bridged
The catch: on your own chain you can deploy a copy of the USDC contract, but those tokens are worth nothing unless a trusted issuer backs them. Value comes from the reserve and the redemption promise, not from the code. Interoperability with real liquidity needs bridges or a rollup that settles to mainnet.

08 · Throughput

A stablecoin has no TPS. Its chain does.

A USDC transfer is just a transaction on whatever chain it lives on. On Ethereum mainnet it competes for block space with everything else.

Limited by Ethereum? On L1, yes. Blocks come every 12 seconds with a gas limit. An ERC-20 transfer costs about 35k to 65k gas, so mainnet handles on the order of tens of transfers per second, shared with DeFi, NFTs and everything else. To scale you move the stablecoin to where block space is cheap.

1 · Layer 2 rollups

Execute off L1, settle on L1

Base, Arbitrum, Optimism batch thousands of transfers and post compressed data to Ethereum. Blobs (EIP-4844) made this much cheaper. Fees drop to cents or less.

2 · Multi chain

Issue natively on fast chains

Circle mints USDC directly on Solana, Base and others, and CCTP moves it between them. Volume spreads across many chains instead of one.

3 · Stablecoin chains

Purpose built L1s

Chains optimized for payments: Circle's Arc, Stripe's Tempo, Plasma for USDT. Stablecoin as gas, fast finality, high throughput.

4 · Off chain netting

Batch and settle

Exchanges, custodians and payment processors move balances in an internal database and only settle net amounts on chain. Payment channels follow the same idea.

5 · L1 upgrades

Bigger Ethereum blocks

Ethereum keeps raising the gas limit and blob capacity, and parallel execution and faster clients (like Reth) push L1 further. Still, L1 is the settlement layer, not the payments layer.
